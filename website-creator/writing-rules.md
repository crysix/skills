# Writing rules for website copy

Every site has its own voice, and the user sets it. These rules don't prescribe a style. They only take out what gives AI-written text away. No single pattern is forbidden; what is suspicious is when they pile up.

The rules apply in any language. The examples are in English; the notes on German pages cover what is specific to German.

## Content

- **Concrete, not grand.** "We look at where your business loses time" instead of "We revolutionize your processes".
- **Buttons say what happens:** "Request a project", "Pick a date", not "Get started".
- **One statement per sentence.** Short sentences, but not choppy.
- **Invent nothing.** No numbers, quotes, awards or sources the user didn't provide. No promises on their behalf either, such as "I'll get back to you within two business days" or "free and without obligation". If a fact is missing, collect it as an open question.
- **The user's verbatim wording** is used verbatim.

## Patterns that sound like AI

**Words and phrases**
- Inflated significance: "plays a crucial role", "an important milestone", "in today's fast-paced world". Say what concretely happens instead.
- Advertising language without proof: "first-class", "unique", "with passion", "your strong partner".
- Buzzwords: seamless, tailored, holistic, innovative, dive into, landscape (as a metaphor), unleash, take it to the next level. On German pages: nahtlos, maßgeschneidert, ganzheitlich, innovativ, eintauchen, Landschaft, entfesseln, auf das nächste Level.
- Vague authorities: "studies show", "experts agree". Either name them concretely or leave them out.
- Sentences that announce their own importance: "It's important to understand that …".

**Sentence structure**
- Dashes, that is em or en dashes used as punctuation. Use a comma, period or colon instead.
- "not only …, but also …" and "it's not about X, it's about Y".
- Groups of three for emphasis: "fast, secure and efficient".
- Dodging "is" and "has": "serves as", "functions as", "represents", "boasts". The plain sentence is better.
- Tacked-on interpretations: "…, which underlines the quality", "…, building trust".
- Paragraphs that mechanically start with "Furthermore", "Additionally" or "Moreover".
- Compulsively alternating synonyms. The same word may repeat if it is the right one.
- Rhetorical questions as an opener: "Sound familiar?".

**Structure and form**
- Labels with a colon before a sentence ("The result: …", "In short: …") and lists following the pattern "**Keyword:** explanation".
- Paragraphs that are all built the same way.
- Closing paragraphs that only repeat what was already said.
- Headings that sell instead of name.
- Lots of bold text, emojis before headings or list items.

## Form

- Keep the form of address consistent: formal or informal, never mixed.
- Use the language's own quotation marks consistently, not mixed with straight ones.
- No placeholder text ("[insert name]") in finished copy.

On German pages:
- Real umlauts and ß, never written as ae, oe, ue or ss. Exception: Swiss pages never use ß, always ss ([countries/ch.md](countries/ch.md)).
- Sie or du throughout, never mixed.
- German quotation marks („so"), not mixed with straight ones. Swiss pages use guillemets («so»).

## Delicate sentences

Sentences about demand, selectivity or waiting times quickly sound arrogant ("Due to the high number of inquiries, it may take a while before you hear from me"). Best let the user write such sentences themselves and only smooth the language.

## Quick check before handing over

- Is there a dash anywhere?
- Is there advertising or significance language that can go?
- Is every number and every claim backed by the user?
- Does a sentence sound like it could be on any other site in the industry?

If yes, revise.
